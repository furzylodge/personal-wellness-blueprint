# Personal Wellness Blueprint
## Methodology Checkpoint

**Version:** 0.3 checkpoint
**Status:** Evidence-led design methodology; not a claim of formal clinical validation

## Purpose

PWB is designed to provide a personalised wellness profile from questionnaire responses, contextual information and recommendation constraints. It is not intended to diagnose disease.

## Development approach

### 1. Define the constructs

PWB begins with nine core wellness outcomes and defines what each outcome is and is not intended to represent.

### 2. Review questions individually

Each question is reviewed for:

- construct relevance
- clarity
- specificity
- recall period where applicable
- redundancy
- applicability
- evidence support
- recommendation usefulness

### 3. Modernise where necessary

Questions are retained, rewritten, split, removed or replaced where the original wording is weak, outdated, double-barrelled or not sufficiently actionable.

Examples include quantified alcohol exposure, modern digital/social behaviour, sedentary behaviour, UPF exposure, sleep/circadian routine and redesigned physical-activity measurement.

### 4. Separate evidence roles

PWB distinguishes:

- direct evidence
- supporting evidence
- context
- modifiers
- safety constraints

This prevents goals, medical conditions, family history or safety information from being mistaken for current wellness deficits.

### 5. Use conditional applicability

Questions are shown only where relevant. Biological/life-stage-specific questions are not presented universally, and evidence applicability is kept separate from question applicability.

### 6. Control redundancy

Questions that describe overlapping underlying constructs are grouped into convergence clusters so that repeated manifestations of the same issue do not create artificially large scores.

### 7. Add contextual relevance without manufacturing need

Age and family health history modify interpretation and priority. They do not add arbitrary disease points.

### 8. Separate outcomes from recommendations

The intended chain is:

```text
Questionnaire evidence
 -> outcome signal
 -> priority
 -> mechanisms
 -> foods/products
 -> evidence and safety filtering
```

A recommendation does not prove a diagnosis, and a product relationship does not itself create a health score.

### 9. Test before freeze

The model should be tested against synthetic acceptance profiles covering balanced, sleep-led, stress-led, digestive-led, lifestyle-led, multi-signal, context-heavy, respiratory-led, goal-led and minimal-signal cases.

The historical-derived respondent dataset can then be used for sanity checks, redundancy analysis and distribution inspection. It is not the source of truth for new weights and is not clinical validation.

## Public description

Appropriate public language:

- evidence-informed
- evidence-led
- research-informed
- designed around established health and lifestyle evidence

Avoid claiming:

- clinically validated
- medically validated
- diagnostic
- doctor-level assessment
- prediction of future disease

until appropriate formal validation evidence exists.

## Future empirical validation

Once a sufficiently large respondent dataset exists, future work can examine:

- response distributions
- internal consistency where appropriate
- dimensionality / structural validity
- convergent and discriminant validity
- test-retest reliability
- known-group differences
- relationships with relevant external measures

The current project stage is questionnaire/content development and scoring-model calibration, not formal clinical validation.
