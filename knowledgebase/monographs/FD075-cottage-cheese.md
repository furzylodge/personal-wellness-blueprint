# FD075 - Cottage Cheese

**Knowledge Base ID:** FD075\
**Scientific Name:** *Bos taurus* (dairy)\
**Common Names:** Cottage Cheese\
**Food Group:** Dairy (FG010)\
**Evidence Level:** High\
**Status:** Approved

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Overview

Cottage cheese is a fresh, soft, unripened cheese made from milk curds, valued for its high protein content relative to calories and its comparatively mild flavour and texture. It is notably rich in casein, a slow-digesting protein.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Why It Matters

Cottage cheese consumption contributes to:

-   High protein intake with relatively low calorie density
-   Calcium intake supporting bone health
-   Slow-release casein protein, relevant to sustained amino acid availability

Its high protein-to-calorie ratio makes it popular within weight-management and higher-protein dietary patterns.

# Active Compounds

## Casein Protein

Cottage cheese is particularly rich in casein, a slow-digesting milk protein associated with a sustained release of amino acids into the bloodstream over several hours.

## Calcium

Contributes to bone mineralisation, though generally at a lower concentration per serving than hard cheeses such as cheddar due to its higher moisture content.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Health Benefits

## Muscle Maintenance and Recovery

The slow-digesting casein protein in cottage cheese is often used to support overnight muscle protein synthesis and recovery.

## Weight Management Support

Its high protein and comparatively low calorie and fat content (particularly reduced-fat varieties) can support satiety within calorie-controlled eating patterns.

## Bone Health

Calcium content contributes to bone mineralisation as part of a balanced diet.

# Suitable For

-   Egg Free
-   Soy Free
-   Nut Free
-   Gluten Free
-   Vegetarian (if made with non-animal rennet)
-   Pregnancy (when made from pasteurised milk)

Not suitable for: Vegan diets. Not suitable for individuals with lactose intolerance or cow's milk protein allergy.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Allergen Information

Milk is one of the fourteen major allergens requiring mandatory labelling in the UK/EU.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Contraindications

**Avoid if:**

-   Confirmed cow's milk protein allergy
-   Made from unpasteurised milk, during pregnancy (check labelling)

**Use caution if:**

-   Lactose intolerant, as cottage cheese retains more lactose than hard, aged cheeses
-   Managing sodium intake, as some varieties are higher in added salt

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Practical Guidance

## Buying

Choose:

-   Pasteurised varieties, particularly during pregnancy
-   Lower-sodium options where available

## Storage

Refrigerate and consume within the use-by date once opened.

## Preparation

Suitable eaten on its own, with fruit, in salads, or as a protein addition to savoury or sweet dishes.

# Common Myth

**Myth:** Cottage cheese and hard cheeses provide the same amount of calcium per serving.

**Fact:** Because cottage cheese is much higher in moisture than hard, aged cheeses like cheddar, it generally provides considerably less calcium per equivalent weight.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Key Takeaway

Cottage cheese is a high-protein, moderate-calcium dairy food particularly valued for its slow-digesting casein content, useful within weight-management and higher-protein dietary patterns.
