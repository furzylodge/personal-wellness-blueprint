# FD086 - Pears

**Common Names:** Pears\
**Food Group:** Fruit (FG003)\
**Evidence Level:** High\
**Status:** Approved

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Overview

Pears are a fibre-rich fruit notable for one of the higher fibre contents among common fruits (particularly with skin retained), along with vitamin C and a naturally high water content.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Why It Matters

Pears offer a useful, widely accessible source of dietary fibre:

-   Among the higher-fibre common fruits when eaten with skin
-   Contains both soluble and insoluble fibre
-   Good source of vitamin C and potassium
-   High water content, contributing to hydration and satiety

# Active Compounds

## Dietary Fibre

Pears provide a mix of soluble and insoluble fibre, concentrated particularly in the skin, supporting digestive regularity and satiety.

## Sorbitol

A natural sugar alcohol present in pears in higher amounts than many other fruits, which can have a mild laxative effect and contribute to gas or bloating if consumed in large quantities by sensitive individuals.

## Polyphenols

Pears contain various polyphenols, including chlorogenic acid, concentrated mainly in the skin, contributing antioxidant activity.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Health Benefits

## Digestive Health

The combined fibre and sorbitol content supports bowel regularity; pears are commonly recommended as a gentle option for constipation, including in some paediatric contexts.

## Satiety and Weight Management

High water and fibre content contribute to fullness relative to the fruit's calorie content.

## Cardiovascular and Metabolic Health

As with other fibre-rich fruits, regular consumption is associated in cohort studies with favourable cardiovascular and metabolic outcomes as part of an overall dietary pattern.

# Allergen Information

Pears are not one of the fourteen major EU/UK allergens, though oral allergy syndrome (mild, pollen-related cross-reactivity) can occur in some individuals sensitive to birch pollen.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Contraindications

**Avoid if:**

-   Confirmed pear allergy or significant oral allergy syndrome reaction

**Use caution if:**

-   Individuals with irritable bowel syndrome or fructose/sorbitol sensitivity may experience bloating or discomfort from pears' natural sorbitol content, particularly in larger portions

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Practical Guidance

## Buying

Choose:

-   Pears at a range of ripeness; they continue to ripen after picking and are often sold slightly underripe
-   Eating with skin intact where possible to maximise fibre intake

## Storage

Store at room temperature to ripen, then refrigerate to slow further ripening once at desired firmness.

## Preparation

Suitable eaten whole, sliced into salads, poached, baked, or stewed.

# Common Myth

**Myth:** All the nutritional value of a pear is in the flesh, not the skin.

**Fact:** Pear skin contains a substantial proportion of the fruit's total fibre and polyphenol content, so peeling significantly reduces its nutritional value.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Key Takeaway

Pears are an accessible, fibre-rich fruit particularly useful for digestive regularity, with most of their fibre and polyphenol content concentrated in the skin.
