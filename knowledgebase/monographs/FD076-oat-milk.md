# FD076 - Oat Milk

**Knowledge Base ID:** FD076\
**Scientific Name:** *Avena sativa* (beverage)\
**Common Names:** Oat Milk, Oat Drink\
**Food Group:** Plant-Based Alternatives (FG013)\
**Evidence Level:** Moderate\
**Status:** Approved

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Overview

Oat milk is a plant-based dairy alternative made by blending oats with water and straining the mixture. It naturally retains some soluble fibre (beta-glucan) from oats, and commercial products are typically fortified with calcium, vitamin D and vitamin B12 to nutritionally resemble cow's milk.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Why It Matters

Oat milk is a widely used dairy alternative for those avoiding dairy for reasons of allergy, intolerance or preference. Its nutritional value depends heavily on fortification:

-   Unfortified oat milk is comparatively low in calcium, vitamin D and vitamin B12
-   Most commercial oat milk is fortified to bring these in line with cow's milk
-   Retains some beta-glucan soluble fibre from oats

# Active Compounds

## Beta-glucan

A soluble fibre carried over from oats, though present in oat milk at considerably lower concentration than in whole oats due to the processing and straining involved.

## Fortificants (Calcium, Vitamin D, Vitamin B12)

Added during manufacturing in most commercial products to match the nutrient profile of cow's milk; unfortified products lack these in meaningful amounts.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Health Benefits

## Dairy-Free Alternative

Provides a plant-based option for those with lactose intolerance, cow's milk protein allergy, or following a vegan diet.

## Cardiovascular Support (via beta-glucan)

The beta-glucan retained from oats may offer modest support for cholesterol management, though at a much lower dose than eating whole oats directly.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Allergen Information

Oats are not one of the fourteen major EU/UK allergens but are frequently processed alongside gluten-containing grains; check labelling for "gluten-free" certification if required.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Contraindications

**Avoid if:**

-   Confirmed oat allergy
-   Coeliac disease, unless made from certified gluten-free oats

**Use caution if:**

-   Relying on oat milk as a primary calcium/vitamin D/B12 source without checking it is fortified, as unfortified products are considerably lower in these nutrients than dairy milk

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Practical Guidance

## Buying

Choose:

-   Fortified varieties (calcium, vitamin D, vitamin B12) for closer nutritional parity with dairy milk
-   Certified gluten-free oat milk if coeliac or gluten-sensitive
-   Unsweetened varieties to limit added sugar

## Storage

Refrigerate after opening and use within the recommended timeframe; check for shelf-stable vs. chilled variants.

## Preparation

Suitable for drinking, in tea/coffee, cereal, baking and cooking.

# Common Myth

**Myth:** Oat milk is nutritionally identical to cow's milk.

**Fact:** Unfortified oat milk is considerably lower in protein, calcium, vitamin D and vitamin B12 than cow's milk; fortified products close much of this gap but protein content generally remains lower.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Key Takeaway

Oat milk is a widely available dairy alternative whose nutritional value depends heavily on fortification; fortified varieties offer a reasonable calcium, vitamin D and B12 substitute for those avoiding dairy.
