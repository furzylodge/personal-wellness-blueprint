# FD071 - Turkey

**Knowledge Base ID:** FD071\
**Scientific Name:** *Meleagris gallopavo*\
**Common Names:** Turkey Breast, Turkey Meat\
**Food Group:** Meat & Poultry (FG008)\
**Evidence Level:** High\
**Status:** Approved

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Overview

Turkey is a lean poultry meat, particularly the breast, known for its high protein content and low fat content relative to many other meats. It is a good source of several B vitamins, selenium and the amino acid tryptophan.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Why It Matters

Turkey consumption is associated with:

-   High-quality complete protein intake
-   A lean alternative to fattier meats
-   Meaningful selenium, niacin and vitamin B6 content
-   Tryptophan intake, a precursor to serotonin and melatonin synthesis

# Active Compounds

## Tryptophan

An essential amino acid and precursor to serotonin and melatonin. Turkey is often popularly associated with drowsiness due to its tryptophan content, though the effect in practice is modest and confounded by meal size and carbohydrate intake.

## Selenium

Supports antioxidant defence via selenoproteins and normal thyroid hormone metabolism.

## Niacin and Vitamin B6

Involved in energy metabolism and amino acid metabolism respectively.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Health Benefits

## Muscle Maintenance

As with other lean poultry, turkey's complete protein profile supports muscle maintenance and repair.

## Metabolic Health

Low fat content (in skinless breast) makes turkey a useful lean protein choice within calorie-controlled or heart-healthy dietary patterns.

## Immune and Thyroid Support

Selenium contributes to normal immune function and thyroid hormone metabolism.

# Allergen Information

Poultry is not one of the recognised major food allergens, though allergy, while rare, can occur.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Contraindications

**Avoid if:**

-   Confirmed poultry allergy

**Use caution if:**

-   Processed turkey products (e.g. deli/luncheon meats) are consumed frequently, due to higher sodium and preservative content compared with fresh turkey

Thorough cooking is essential to reduce risk of bacterial contamination.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Practical Guidance

## Buying

Choose:

-   Fresh or frozen turkey breast
-   Minimally processed cuts over deli/luncheon turkey where possible

## Storage

Refrigerate raw turkey and use within the use-by date, or freeze for longer storage.

## Preparation

Cook thoroughly until no longer pink and juices run clear. Suitable for roasting, grilling and stir-frying.

# Common Myth

**Myth:** Turkey's tryptophan content is uniquely responsible for post-meal drowsiness.

**Fact:** Many protein foods contain comparable tryptophan levels; post-meal sleepiness is more strongly linked to overall meal size and carbohydrate content.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Key Takeaway

Turkey is a lean, versatile source of complete protein, selenium and B vitamins, making it a useful addition to a balanced diet, particularly when fresh rather than heavily processed.
