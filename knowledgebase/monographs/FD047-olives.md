# FD047 - Olives

**Knowledge Base ID:** FD047\
**Scientific Name:** *Olea europaea*\
**Common Names:** Olives\
**Food Group:** Fruit (FG003)\
**Evidence Level:** High\
**Status:** Approved

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Overview

Olives are the fruit of the olive tree and a cornerstone food of the Mediterranean diet, valued for their monounsaturated fat content and distinctive polyphenols, most notably oleocanthal and hydroxytyrosol.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Why It Matters

Olives are consistently featured in one of the most extensively studied dietary patterns in nutrition science:

-   Rich in monounsaturated fat, primarily oleic acid
-   Contain polyphenols including hydroxytyrosol and oleocanthal, studied for anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties
-   A core component of the Mediterranean dietary pattern, itself linked to favourable cardiovascular outcomes in large studies
-   Typically consumed with a meaningful sodium load due to curing/brining, which is worth factoring into overall intake

# Active Compounds

## Oleic Acid

The predominant fatty acid in olives, a monounsaturated fat associated with favourable blood lipid profiles when used to replace saturated fat.

## Hydroxytyrosol and Oleocanthal

Polyphenols concentrated in olives (and olive oil), studied for antioxidant and anti-inflammatory activity; oleocanthal has drawn particular research interest for a mechanism of action resembling that of ibuprofen, though at much lower potency from dietary intake.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Health Benefits

## Cardiovascular Health

As part of the broader Mediterranean dietary pattern, regular olive and olive oil consumption is associated with favourable cardiovascular outcomes in large observational and interventional studies (e.g. PREDIMED).

## Antioxidant Support

Polyphenol content contributes to overall dietary antioxidant intake.

# Allergen Information

Olives are not one of the fourteen major EU/UK allergens; allergy is rare.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Contraindications

**Avoid if:**

-   Confirmed olive allergy (rare)

**Use caution if:**

-   Following a reduced-sodium diet, as brined/cured olives (the most common commercial form) can contain a substantial amount of added salt per serving; rinsing or choosing lower-sodium products can help

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Practical Guidance

## Buying

Choose:

-   Olives cured in brine or oil rather than heavily processed varieties with added preservatives, where possible
-   Lower-sodium options if managing salt intake

## Storage

Store in their brine or oil in the refrigerator once opened; use within the recommended timeframe on packaging.

## Preparation

Suitable eaten whole as a snack or appetiser, chopped into salads, tapenades, and Mediterranean-style dishes.

# Common Myth

**Myth:** Olives are mainly a source of fat and offer little else nutritionally.

**Fact:** Alongside their monounsaturated fat content, olives provide meaningful polyphenol antioxidants and vitamin E, contributing to the broader health associations seen with the Mediterranean dietary pattern.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Key Takeaway

Olives are a polyphenol-rich, monounsaturated-fat food with a strong evidence base as part of the Mediterranean dietary pattern, though their sodium content from curing is worth factoring into overall intake.
