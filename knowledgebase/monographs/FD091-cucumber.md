# FD091 - Cucumber

**Knowledge Base ID:** FD091\
**Scientific Name:** *Cucumis sativus*\
**Common Names:** Cucumber\
**Food Group:** Vegetables (FG002)\
**Evidence Level:** Moderate\
**Status:** Approved

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Overview

Cucumber is a high-water-content vegetable (around 95% water), providing modest but useful amounts of vitamin K and contributing to hydration and low-calorie bulk within meals.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Why It Matters

While not a nutrient powerhouse compared with denser vegetables, cucumber has a distinct role:

-   Very high water content supports hydration
-   Provides vitamin K, relevant to blood clotting and bone health
-   Very low in calories, useful for adding volume and crunch to meals with minimal energy contribution
-   Contains skin-concentrated fibre and some antioxidant compounds

# Active Compounds

## Vitamin K

Cucumber, particularly the skin, provides a useful contribution to vitamin K intake, relevant to normal blood clotting function.

## Cucurbitacins

Bitter-tasting compounds found mainly in the skin and stem end, present at low levels in commercially available cucumbers; responsible for occasional bitterness and studied in preclinical research for various bioactivities, though not established as clinically significant at typical dietary intakes.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Health Benefits

## Hydration

Cucumber's very high water content makes it a useful contributor to overall fluid intake alongside drinking water.

## Low-Calorie Bulk

Its low energy density makes cucumber a useful addition to meals and salads for volume and satiety without a substantial calorie contribution.

## Bone and Blood Clotting Support

Vitamin K content, primarily in the skin, contributes to normal blood clotting and has a role in bone metabolism.

# Allergen Information

Cucumber is not one of the fourteen major EU/UK allergens, though mild oral allergy syndrome can occur in individuals sensitive to certain pollens (cross-reactivity with ragweed has been noted).

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Contraindications

**Avoid if:**

-   Confirmed cucumber allergy (uncommon)

**Use caution if:**

-   Taking vitamin K-sensitive anticoagulant medication (e.g. warfarin), where consistent vitamin K intake, including from cucumber skin, should be discussed with a healthcare provider

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Practical Guidance

## Buying

Choose:

-   Firm cucumbers with smooth, unblemished skin

## Storage

Store in the refrigerator; use within about a week, as cucumbers can become soft over time.

## Preparation

Suitable raw in salads, sandwiches, or as a snack with dips; retaining the skin (washed well) provides more fibre and vitamin K than peeled cucumber.

# Common Myth

**Myth:** Cucumber has no real nutritional value because it's "mostly water."

**Fact:** While cucumber is low in calories and mostly water, it still provides meaningful hydration and useful vitamin K, particularly from the skin, making it a valuable low-energy-density addition to the diet rather than a nutritionally empty food.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Key Takeaway

Cucumber is a hydrating, low-calorie vegetable that contributes useful vitamin K and adds valuable bulk to meals without a substantial energy cost.
