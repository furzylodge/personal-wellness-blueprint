# FD049 - Spirulina

**Knowledge Base ID:** FD049\
**Scientific Name:** *Arthrospira platensis*\
**Common Names:** Spirulina\
**Food Group:** Algae & Superfoods (FG016)\
**Evidence Level:** Moderate\
**Status:** Approved

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Overview

Spirulina is a blue-green cyanobacterium consumed as a dried powder or supplement, notable for its very high protein content, phycocyanin pigment, and dense micronutrient profile, though quality and safety depend heavily on cultivation and testing standards.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Why It Matters

Spirulina has drawn substantial research and commercial interest for its nutrient density:

-   Exceptionally high protein content by dry weight, including all essential amino acids
-   Rich in phycocyanin, a unique blue-green antioxidant pigment
-   Good source of iron and some B vitamins, though vitamin B12 in spirulina is largely present as an inactive analogue and should not be relied upon as a B12 source
-   Product quality varies significantly; contamination with heavy metals or microcystins is a recognised risk in poorly regulated products

# Active Compounds

## Phycocyanin

A blue-green pigment-protein complex unique to spirulina and related cyanobacteria, studied in preclinical research for antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties; robust large-scale human clinical evidence remains limited.

## Protein

Spirulina's protein content by dry weight is unusually high for a plant-based food and includes all essential amino acids, though typical serving sizes (a few grams as a supplement) provide a modest absolute protein contribution compared with whole-food protein sources.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Health Benefits

## Nutrient Density in Small Servings

Spirulina's concentrated protein, iron and antioxidant content can make it a useful minor contributor within a varied diet, particularly for plant-based eating patterns, though it should not replace whole-food protein and iron sources.

## Antioxidant Support

Phycocyanin and other pigments contribute antioxidant activity, an active area of ongoing research.

# Allergen Information

Spirulina is not one of the fourteen major EU/UK allergens, though allergic reactions have been reported, and cross-reactivity in individuals with seafood/shellfish allergy has been noted in some case reports.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Contraindications

**Avoid if:**

-   Confirmed spirulina allergy
-   Autoimmune conditions, without medical advice, as spirulina may stimulate immune activity and its interaction with immunosuppressive treatment is not well established
-   Phenylketonuria (some spirulina products may be relevant to phenylalanine intake; check product-specific advice)

**Use caution if:**

-   Choosing a supplement brand: buy from reputable manufacturers who test for heavy metal and microcystin (cyanotoxin) contamination, as poorly regulated spirulina products have been associated with contamination risks
-   Relying on spirulina as a source of vitamin B12; the B12 analogues present are not reliably bioactive in humans

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Practical Guidance

## Buying

Choose:

-   Products from reputable manufacturers with third-party contaminant testing (heavy metals, microcystins)
-   Powder or tablet form based on preference; taste is often described as strong and "oceanic"

## Storage

Store in an airtight container away from light and moisture, per packaging guidance.

## Preparation

Suitable stirred into smoothies, juices, or added in small amounts to foods; typically used in gram-level quantities rather than as a bulk food.

# Common Myth

**Myth:** Spirulina is a reliable source of vitamin B12 for vegans.

**Fact:** While spirulina contains B12-like compounds, most are inactive analogues that are not usable by the body and may even interfere with the measurement or absorption of genuine B12; vegans should rely on fortified foods or supplements for B12 rather than spirulina.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Key Takeaway

Spirulina is a nutrient-dense, protein-rich algae supplement with promising but still-developing evidence for its antioxidant compounds; product quality and contaminant testing matter considerably, and it should not be relied upon as a vitamin B12 source.
