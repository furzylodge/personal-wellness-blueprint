# FD089 - Potato

**Knowledge Base ID:** FD089\
**Scientific Name:** *Solanum tuberosum*\
**Common Names:** Potato, White Potato\
**Food Group:** Vegetables (FG002)\
**Evidence Level:** High\
**Status:** Approved

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Overview

Potato is a starchy tuber vegetable and a dietary staple in many countries, providing meaningful potassium and vitamin C, particularly when the skin is retained. Its glycaemic and nutritional impact varies considerably with cooking and preparation method.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Why It Matters

Despite sometimes being viewed simply as a starch source, potatoes contribute meaningfully to several nutrients:

-   One of the richest common vegetable sources of potassium
-   Contains useful vitamin C, especially when cooked with skin on
-   Cooling cooked potato before eating increases resistant starch content, altering its digestive and glycaemic profile
-   Nutrient and glycaemic impact depends heavily on preparation (boiled/baked with skin vs. fried/mashed with added fats)

# Active Compounds

## Potassium

Potatoes, particularly with skin retained, are a rich dietary source of potassium, relevant to blood pressure regulation.

## Resistant Starch

Cooking and then cooling potatoes (e.g. for potato salad) converts some of the starch into resistant starch, which behaves more like fibre in the gut and is associated with a lower glycaemic response than freshly cooked, hot potato.

## Vitamin C

Present in useful amounts, particularly just beneath the skin; retained better with boiling or baking than with prolonged high-heat frying.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Health Benefits

## Blood Pressure Support

Potassium content, especially from potatoes eaten with skin, contributes to diets supporting healthy blood pressure.

## Digestive Health

Fibre (with skin) and resistant starch (when cooled after cooking) support digestive health and beneficial gut bacteria.

## Satiety

Boiled potatoes have been noted in some satiety research as comparatively filling relative to their calorie content, though preparation method (e.g. added fats when fried) substantially affects this.

# Allergen Information

Potato is not one of the fourteen major EU/UK allergens; allergy is uncommon.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Contraindications

**Avoid if:**

-   Confirmed potato allergy (uncommon)

**Use caution if:**

-   Potatoes that have turned green or sprouted should be discarded or trimmed generously, as green skin and sprouts contain elevated levels of solanine, a naturally occurring compound that can be toxic in larger amounts

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Practical Guidance

## Buying

Choose:

-   Firm potatoes without green discolouration, sprouting, or soft spots

## Storage

Store in a cool, dark, well-ventilated place away from light, which encourages sprouting and greening; do not refrigerate, as cold storage can adversely affect texture and flavour when cooked.

## Preparation

Boiling or baking with the skin on retains the most nutrients and results in a lower glycaemic impact than frying or mashing with added fats; cooling cooked potatoes before eating increases resistant starch content.

# Common Myth

**Myth:** Potatoes are nutritionally empty "junk carbs."

**Fact:** Potatoes, particularly boiled or baked with the skin retained, provide meaningful potassium, vitamin C and fibre; their reputation as a less healthy food often reflects preparation methods (frying, heavy use of added fats) rather than the vegetable itself.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Key Takeaway

Potato is a versatile, potassium-rich vegetable whose nutritional and glycaemic profile depends heavily on preparation, with boiling or baking (skin on) offering the most favourable nutrient retention and glycaemic response.
