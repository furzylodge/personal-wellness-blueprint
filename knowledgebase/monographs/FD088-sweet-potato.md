# FD088 - Sweet Potato

**Knowledge Base ID:** FD088\
**Scientific Name:** *Ipomoea batatas*\
**Common Names:** Sweet Potato\
**Food Group:** Vegetables (FG002)\
**Evidence Level:** High\
**Status:** Approved

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Overview

Sweet potato is a starchy root vegetable, botanically unrelated to the potato, notable for its exceptionally high beta-carotene content (particularly orange-fleshed varieties) and a naturally sweet flavour.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Why It Matters

Sweet potato stands out among starchy vegetables for its micronutrient density:

-   Among the richest common food sources of beta-carotene (provitamin A)
-   Good source of fibre, particularly with skin retained
-   Contains manganese, potassium and vitamin C
-   Generally has a lower glycaemic response than white potato, though this varies with cooking method

# Active Compounds

## Beta-carotene

Orange-fleshed sweet potato is one of the richest dietary sources of beta-carotene, a precursor to vitamin A important for vision, immune function and skin health; purple-fleshed varieties instead contain anthocyanins.

## Fibre

Present in meaningful amounts, particularly in the skin, supporting digestive health and contributing to a comparatively moderate glycaemic response for a starchy vegetable.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Health Benefits

## Vision and Immune Support

High beta-carotene content supports normal vision and immune function once converted to vitamin A in the body.

## Digestive Health

Fibre content, especially with skin retained, supports bowel regularity.

## Blood Sugar Management

Sweet potato generally produces a comparatively moderate glycaemic response, particularly when boiled rather than baked or roasted, though this varies by preparation method and portion.

# Allergen Information

Sweet potato is not one of the fourteen major EU/UK allergens; allergy is rare.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Contraindications

**Avoid if:**

-   Confirmed sweet potato allergy (rare)

**Use caution if:**

-   Following a low-oxalate diet, as sweet potato (particularly the skin) contains oxalates that may be relevant for those prone to certain types of kidney stones

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Practical Guidance

## Buying

Choose:

-   Firm sweet potatoes without soft spots or sprouting
-   Orange-fleshed varieties for the highest beta-carotene content

## Storage

Store in a cool, dark, well-ventilated place rather than the refrigerator, which can negatively affect texture and flavour.

## Preparation

Suitable baked, roasted, boiled, or mashed; cooking with the skin on and boiling rather than baking can help moderate glycaemic response and retain nutrients.

# Common Myth

**Myth:** Sweet potatoes and potatoes are nutritionally interchangeable, just different colours of the same food.

**Fact:** Sweet potatoes and potatoes are botanically unrelated plants; sweet potato is notably higher in beta-carotene, while white potato is notably higher in potassium, and their glycaemic responses differ depending on preparation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Key Takeaway

Sweet potato is a nutrient-dense starchy vegetable, particularly valued for its exceptional beta-carotene content, and generally offers a more moderate glycaemic response than white potato depending on preparation.
