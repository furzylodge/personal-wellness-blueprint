# FD073 - Milk

**Knowledge Base ID:** FD073\
**Scientific Name:** *Bos taurus* (dairy)\
**Common Names:** Cow's Milk, Dairy Milk\
**Food Group:** Dairy (FG010)\
**Evidence Level:** High\
**Status:** Approved

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Overview

Milk is a nutrient-dense liquid food naturally rich in calcium, protein, iodine and, in many countries, fortified with vitamin D. It is a foundational food in many dietary patterns, particularly for bone health during childhood, adolescence and later life.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Why It Matters

Regular milk consumption, as part of a balanced diet, is associated with:

-   Meaningful contributions to calcium intake, supporting bone mineralisation
-   High-quality complete protein (casein and whey)
-   Iodine intake, important for thyroid function
-   Vitamin D intake where fortified, supporting calcium absorption

# Active Compounds

## Casein and Whey Proteins

Milk's two principal protein fractions, both considered high-quality complete proteins containing all essential amino acids.

## Calcium

Milk is one of the most significant dietary sources of calcium in many Western diets, important for bone and teeth mineralisation and for normal muscle and nerve function.

## Iodine

Milk and dairy products are a major dietary source of iodine in the UK and similar diets, essential for thyroid hormone production.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Health Benefits

## Bone Health

Calcium and (where fortified) vitamin D content support bone mineral density, particularly important during growth and in later life to help reduce osteoporosis risk.

## Muscle Maintenance

High-quality protein content supports muscle maintenance and repair.

## Thyroid Function

Iodine content contributes to normal thyroid hormone production.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Allergen Information

Milk is one of the fourteen major allergens requiring mandatory labelling in the UK/EU. Cow's milk protein allergy and lactose intolerance are distinct conditions; lactose-free milk is available for those with lactose intolerance.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Contraindications

**Avoid if:**

-   Confirmed cow's milk protein allergy
-   Galactosaemia

**Use caution if:**

-   Lactose intolerant (lactose-free alternatives may be suitable)

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Practical Guidance

## Buying

Choose:

-   Pasteurised milk from a reputable source
-   Fortified varieties where vitamin D fortification is not already mandated locally

## Storage

Refrigerate and consume by the use-by date; do not leave at room temperature for extended periods.

## Preparation

Suitable for drinking, cooking, baking and as a base for sauces, porridge and smoothies.

# Common Myth

**Myth:** Milk is an equally good source of vitamin D everywhere.

**Fact:** Vitamin D content depends on fortification, which varies significantly by country; unfortified milk contains only small amounts of naturally occurring vitamin D.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Key Takeaway

Milk is a nutrient-dense source of calcium, protein and iodine, playing a meaningful role in bone and thyroid health as part of a balanced diet, for those who tolerate it.
