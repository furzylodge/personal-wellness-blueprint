# FD078 - Soy Milk

**Knowledge Base ID:** FD078\
**Scientific Name:** *Glycine max* (beverage)\
**Common Names:** Soy Milk, Soya Milk\
**Food Group:** Plant-Based Alternatives (FG013)\
**Evidence Level:** High\
**Status:** Approved

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Overview

Soy milk is a plant-based dairy alternative made from soaked, ground and strained soybeans. Among plant milks, it is nutritionally the closest to dairy milk in protein content, as soy protein is a complete protein. It also naturally contains isoflavones, plant compounds studied for various health effects.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Why It Matters

Soy milk stands out among plant-based milks for its protein quality:

-   Naturally provides a complete protein comparable in quantity to dairy milk
-   Contains isoflavones, plant compounds with mild oestrogenic activity studied in relation to menopausal symptoms and cardiovascular health
-   Fortified varieties provide calcium, vitamin D and vitamin B12 comparable to dairy milk

# Active Compounds

## Soy Protein

A complete plant protein containing all essential amino acids, unusual among plant-based protein sources and comparable in quantity to dairy milk protein.

## Isoflavones (Genistein, Daidzein)

Plant compounds structurally similar to oestrogen, studied for potential roles in menopausal symptom management and cardiovascular health; research findings on clinical benefit remain mixed and this should be treated as an area of ongoing rather than settled evidence.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Health Benefits

## Protein Intake for Plant-Based Diets

Soy milk's complete protein profile makes it a particularly useful dairy alternative for vegans and vegetarians seeking to maintain protein intake comparable to dairy milk.

## Cardiovascular Health

Soy protein has been associated in some research with modest improvements in LDL cholesterol when substituted for animal protein, though effect sizes are generally modest.

# Allergen Information

Soy (soybeans) is one of the fourteen major EU/UK allergens requiring mandatory labelling.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Contraindications

**Avoid if:**

-   Confirmed soy allergy

**Use caution if:**

-   A history of hormone-sensitive conditions raises questions about isoflavone intake; individual clinical advice is recommended in this case rather than a blanket restriction, as current evidence does not support a general contraindication for typical dietary intake

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Practical Guidance

## Buying

Choose:

-   Fortified, unsweetened varieties for closer nutritional parity with dairy milk without added sugar

## Storage

Refrigerate after opening and use within the recommended timeframe.

## Preparation

Suitable for drinking, in tea/coffee, cereal, baking, cooking and as a substitute in most dairy milk recipes.

# Common Myth

**Myth:** Soy isoflavones act as a strong hormone disruptor at typical dietary intakes.

**Fact:** Current evidence from human studies at typical dietary consumption levels does not support meaningful hormonal disruption; isoflavones have much weaker oestrogenic activity than the body's own oestrogen.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Key Takeaway

Soy milk is the plant-based milk closest to dairy milk in protein quality and quantity, making it a particularly suitable dairy alternative for those wanting to maintain protein intake on a plant-based diet.
