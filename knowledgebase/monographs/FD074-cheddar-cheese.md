# FD074 - Cheddar Cheese

**Knowledge Base ID:** FD074\
**Scientific Name:** *Bos taurus* (dairy)\
**Common Names:** Cheddar, Cheddar Cheese\
**Food Group:** Dairy (FG010)\
**Evidence Level:** High\
**Status:** Approved

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Overview

Cheddar is a hard, aged cow's milk cheese and a concentrated source of calcium, protein and vitamin B12. Because it is a matured, concentrated dairy product, it also tends to be relatively high in saturated fat and sodium compared with fresh milk, so is typically recommended in moderation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Why It Matters

Cheddar cheese, consumed in moderation, contributes to:

-   Concentrated calcium intake, supporting bone health
-   High-quality complete protein intake
-   Vitamin B12 intake, supporting nerve function and red blood cell formation

Because of its saturated fat and sodium content, cheddar is generally recommended as part of a varied diet in moderate portions rather than as a primary dairy source.

# Active Compounds

## Calcium

Cheddar is one of the more calcium-dense dairy foods by weight, due to the concentration process involved in cheesemaking.

## Casein Protein

The predominant milk protein retained in cheese, providing a complete amino acid profile.

## Vitamin K2 (Menaquinone)

Some hard, aged cheeses contain vitamin K2, produced during fermentation, though levels vary considerably by cheese type and production method.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Health Benefits

## Bone Health

Concentrated calcium content supports bone mineralisation when consumed as part of a balanced diet.

## Muscle Maintenance

Complete protein content supports muscle maintenance.

# Suitable For

-   Egg Free
-   Soy Free
-   Nut Free
-   Gluten Free
-   Vegetarian (if made with non-animal rennet)
-   Halal (if made with halal-certified rennet)
-   Pregnancy (when made from pasteurised milk)

Not suitable for: Vegan diets. Not suitable for individuals with lactose intolerance or cow's milk protein allergy (though hard cheeses are naturally lower in lactose than milk).

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Allergen Information

Milk is one of the fourteen major allergens requiring mandatory labelling in the UK/EU.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Contraindications

**Avoid if:**

-   Confirmed cow's milk protein allergy
-   Made from unpasteurised milk, during pregnancy (check labelling)

**Use caution if:**

-   Managing sodium or saturated fat intake for cardiovascular health reasons
-   Lactose intolerant (hard cheeses generally contain less lactose than milk, but individual tolerance varies)

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Practical Guidance

## Buying

Choose:

-   Pasteurised varieties, particularly during pregnancy
-   Reduced-fat versions where lowering saturated fat intake is a goal

## Storage

Refrigerate and consume within the recommended timeframe after opening.

## Preparation

Suitable eaten cold, melted, grated into cooking, or as part of sandwiches and salads.

# Common Myth

**Myth:** All cheese has the same nutritional profile.

**Fact:** Fat, sodium and lactose content vary considerably between cheese types; hard aged cheeses like cheddar are typically higher in calcium and lower in lactose than soft, fresh cheeses.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Key Takeaway

Cheddar cheese is a concentrated source of calcium, protein and vitamin B12, best enjoyed in moderate portions as part of a balanced diet due to its saturated fat and sodium content.
