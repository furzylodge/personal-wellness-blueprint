# FD048 - Chicory Root

**Knowledge Base ID:** FD048\
**Scientific Name:** *Cichorium intybus*\
**Common Names:** Chicory Root\
**Food Group:** Vegetables (FG002)\
**Evidence Level:** High\
**Status:** Approved

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Overview

Chicory root is notable primarily for its exceptionally high inulin content, a soluble prebiotic fibre. It is consumed as a vegetable, used as a coffee substitute/additive when roasted, and widely used as a food-industry fibre ingredient.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Why It Matters

Chicory root's defining nutritional feature is its fibre profile:

-   One of the richest natural sources of inulin, a prebiotic fibre that feeds beneficial gut bacteria
-   Contributes to digestive health and has an EFSA-recognised role in bowel function
-   Naturally caffeine-free when used as a roasted coffee substitute
-   Low in digestible carbohydrate relative to its total fibre content

# Active Compounds

## Inulin

A soluble fibre and fructan present in unusually high concentrations in chicory root, fermented by beneficial gut bacteria in the colon; the European Food Safety Authority has authorised a health claim for chicory inulin and normal bowel function (when adequate daily intake is consumed).

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Health Benefits

## Digestive and Gut Health

Inulin acts as a prebiotic, selectively feeding beneficial gut bacteria such as Bifidobacteria, and supports normal bowel function.

## Blood Sugar Management

As a non-digestible fibre, inulin does not raise blood glucose in the way digestible carbohydrates do, and chicory-based ingredients are sometimes used to replace sugar or flour in lower-glycaemic food products.

# Allergen Information

Chicory root is not one of the fourteen major EU/UK allergens, though it belongs to the same plant family as ragweed and daisies, and cross-reactive allergy can occur in sensitive individuals.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Contraindications

**Avoid if:**

-   Confirmed chicory allergy or known ragweed-family cross-reactivity

**Use caution if:**

-   Introducing high-inulin foods for the first time or in large amounts, as inulin can cause bloating, gas or digestive discomfort in sensitive individuals, particularly those with irritable bowel syndrome (chicory root is high in FODMAPs)

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Practical Guidance

## Buying

Choose:

-   Roasted chicory root as a coffee substitute or additive
-   Chicory-derived inulin fibre ingredients where a prebiotic fibre boost is desired

## Storage

Store roasted chicory products in a cool, dry place per packaging guidance.

## Preparation

Suitable as a roasted, ground coffee substitute or blend; the fresh root/leaf (endive-type chicory) can also be used as a vegetable, typically with a distinctive bitter flavour.

# Common Myth

**Myth:** Chicory root coffee is nutritionally similar to regular coffee, just without caffeine.

**Fact:** Beyond being caffeine-free, chicory root coffee substitute provides a substantial dose of inulin prebiotic fibre, a distinctly different nutritional contribution from regular coffee.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Key Takeaway

Chicory root is one of the richest natural sources of inulin, a well-evidenced prebiotic fibre supporting digestive and gut health, though it can cause digestive discomfort in sensitive individuals or in large amounts.
