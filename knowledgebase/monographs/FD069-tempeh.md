# FD069 - Tempeh

**Knowledge Base ID:** FD069\
**Scientific Name:** *Glycine max* (fermented)\
**Common Names:** Tempeh\
**Food Group:** Beans & Legumes (FG004)\
**Evidence Level:** High\
**Status:** Approved

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Overview

Tempeh is a traditional Indonesian food made by fermenting cooked soybeans with a mould culture (typically *Rhizopus oligosporus*), binding them into a firm, dense cake. Fermentation gives tempeh a distinct nutritional edge over unfermented soy foods, including a firmer texture, more pronounced flavour, and some improvement in nutrient availability.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Why It Matters

Tempeh combines whole soybeans' nutritional strengths with the added benefits of fermentation:

-   Retains soybeans' complete protein profile, and in a more concentrated, less-processed form than some other soy products
-   Fermentation can improve mineral bioavailability by reducing phytic acid content compared with unfermented soybeans
-   Some fermentation strains can generate small amounts of vitamin B12-related compounds, though this varies by production method and should not be relied upon as a consistent B12 source
-   Higher fibre content than tofu, as tempeh uses the whole soybean rather than a strained soy milk product

# Active Compounds

## Complete Protein

Tempeh retains soybeans' full amino acid profile in a dense, minimally processed form.

## Fermentation-Related Compounds

The fermentation process by *Rhizopus* mould can reduce phytic acid (which otherwise binds minerals and reduces their absorption) and may generate small amounts of certain B vitamins; the extent of vitamin B12 production specifically is inconsistent across production methods and should not be relied upon as a dependable dietary B12 source.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Health Benefits

## Plant-Based Protein Support

Tempeh's protein density and completeness make it a particularly useful whole-food protein source within plant-based diets.

## Digestive and Mineral Health

Higher fibre than tofu supports digestive health, and fermentation-related phytic acid reduction may modestly improve mineral absorption compared with unfermented soy.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Contraindications

**Avoid if:**

-   Confirmed soy allergy

**Use caution if:**

-   Relying on tempeh as a vitamin B12 source; production-related B12 content is inconsistent and should not replace fortified foods or supplements for those following a vegan diet
-   Taking thyroid medication, where separating soy food intake from medication timing by a few hours is generally advised

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Practical Guidance

## Buying

Choose:

-   Fresh or frozen tempeh from the refrigerated/frozen section; check for a firm texture with an even white mould coating (occasional black or grey spots are typically harmless and part of natural fermentation)

## Storage

Refrigerate and use within the recommended timeframe on packaging, or freeze for longer storage.

## Preparation

Suitable steamed, marinated, pan-fried, baked, or crumbled into dishes; steaming briefly before other cooking methods can help mellow its naturally slightly bitter flavour and improve marinade absorption.

# Common Myth

**Myth:** Tempeh is a reliable source of vitamin B12 for vegans because it's fermented.

**Fact:** While some fermentation processes can generate small, inconsistent amounts of B12-related compounds, this varies considerably by production method and is not a dependable dietary source; vegans should rely on fortified foods or supplements for consistent B12 intake.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Key Takeaway

Tempeh is a nutrient-dense, minimally processed fermented soy food offering a complete plant protein with a fibre and mineral-availability edge over unfermented soy products, though it should not be relied upon for vitamin B12.
