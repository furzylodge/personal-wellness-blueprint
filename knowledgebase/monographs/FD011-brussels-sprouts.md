# FD011 - Brussels Sprouts

**Knowledge Base ID:** FD011\
**Scientific Name:** *Brassica oleracea var. gemmifera*\
**Common Names:** Brussels Sprouts\
**Food Group:** Vegetables (FG003)\
**Evidence Level:** High\
**Status:** Approved

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Overview

Brussels sprouts are nutrient-dense cruciferous vegetables rich in fibre, vitamins C and K and bioactive glucosinolates associated with cellular protection.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Why It Matters

Brussels Sprouts provides an excellent combination of vitamins, fibre and glucosinolate compounds. Regular consumption of cruciferous vegetables is consistently associated with improved long-term health outcomes and forms an important part of evidence-based dietary patterns.

# Active Compounds

## Glucosinolates

Naturally occurring sulphur-containing compounds that are converted into biologically active metabolites when plant tissues are chopped or chewed.

Research suggests they may:

- Support normal cellular defence mechanisms
- Promote healthy detoxification pathways
- Support healthy inflammatory balance

## Sulforaphane

One of the best studied isothiocyanates found in cruciferous vegetables.

Research suggests it may:

- Activate antioxidant defence pathways
- Support cellular resilience
- Promote healthy ageing

# Practical Guidance

## Buying

Choose firm, brightly coloured produce with no yellowing.

## Storage

Refrigerate and use within several days.

## Preparation

Light steaming helps preserve texture and nutritional quality.

## Shopping Tips

Frozen varieties remain nutritionally valuable and are convenient.

# Common Myth

**Myth**

Raw cruciferous vegetables are always healthier.

**Fact**

Light cooking can improve digestibility while retaining most nutritional value.
