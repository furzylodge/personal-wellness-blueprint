# FD092 - Peas

**Knowledge Base ID:** FD092\
**Scientific Name:** *Pisum sativum*\
**Common Names:** Peas, Green Peas, Garden Peas\
**Food Group:** Beans & Legumes (FG004)\
**Evidence Level:** High\
**Status:** Approved

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Overview

Peas are a legume, though commonly eaten and perceived as a vegetable, offering an unusually favourable combination of plant protein, fibre and vitamin K for a food typically served as a side vegetable.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Why It Matters

Peas sit at a useful intersection between vegetable and legume nutrition:

-   Higher in protein and fibre than most vegetables, reflecting their legume classification
-   Good source of vitamin K and vitamin C
-   Contribute plant protein and fibre even in small side-dish portions
-   Widely available fresh, frozen and tinned, with frozen peas retaining nutrients well

# Active Compounds

## Plant Protein

Peas provide a notably higher protein content than most vegetables, reflecting their botanical classification as a legume, contributing to overall plant protein intake.

## Fibre

Peas provide both soluble and insoluble fibre, supporting digestive health and a more moderate glycaemic response than many other starchy vegetables.

## Vitamin K

Peas are a good source, relevant to normal blood clotting function.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Health Benefits

## Plant-Based Protein Support

Their relatively high protein content for a vegetable-style food makes peas a useful contributor within vegetarian and vegan diets.

## Digestive Health

Fibre content supports bowel regularity and feeds beneficial gut bacteria.

## Blood Sugar Management

The combination of protein and fibre contributes to a comparatively moderate glycaemic response.

# Allergen Information

Peas are not one of the fourteen major EU/UK allergens, though legume allergy, including to peas, is recognised and can occur, particularly in individuals with other legume allergies (e.g. peanut, lentil).

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Contraindications

**Avoid if:**

-   Confirmed pea or legume allergy

**Use caution if:**

-   Taking vitamin K-sensitive anticoagulant medication (e.g. warfarin), where consistent vitamin K intake should be discussed with a healthcare provider

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Practical Guidance

## Buying

Choose:

-   Frozen peas as a convenient, nutrient-retentive option (typically frozen shortly after picking)
-   Fresh peas in season for pods that feel full and firm

## Storage

Keep frozen peas frozen until use; store fresh peas in the refrigerator and use promptly, as sugars convert to starch quickly after picking.

## Preparation

Suitable steamed, boiled briefly, or added to soups, stews, curries and salads; light cooking helps retain vitamin C content.

# Common Myth

**Myth:** Frozen peas are nutritionally inferior to fresh peas.

**Fact:** Frozen peas are typically frozen shortly after harvest, which can preserve nutrients, including vitamin C, as well as or better than "fresh" peas that may have travelled and been stored for longer before reaching the plate.
