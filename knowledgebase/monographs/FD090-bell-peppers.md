# FD090 - Bell Peppers

**Knowledge Base ID:** FD090\
**Scientific Name:** *Capsicum annuum*\
**Common Names:** Bell Peppers, Sweet Peppers, Capsicum\
**Food Group:** Vegetables (FG002)\
**Evidence Level:** High\
**Status:** Approved

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Overview

Bell peppers are a non-pungent (sweet) variety of Capsicum, notable for exceptionally high vitamin C content, especially in red and yellow varieties, along with a range of carotenoids that vary by colour.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Why It Matters

Bell peppers are among the richest common vegetable sources of vitamin C:

-   Red bell peppers contain notably more vitamin C than citrus fruits like oranges, weight for weight
-   Colour reflects ripeness and carotenoid profile: green peppers are less ripe, red are fully ripe and generally highest in vitamin C and carotenoids
-   Low in calories and a good contributor to overall vegetable intake
-   Contains capsanthin and other carotenoids with antioxidant properties

# Active Compounds

## Vitamin C

Bell peppers, particularly red ones, are exceptionally rich in vitamin C, supporting immune function, collagen synthesis and iron absorption from plant foods eaten in the same meal.

## Carotenoids (Capsanthin, Beta-carotene)

Red peppers in particular are rich in capsanthin, a carotenoid pigment, and beta-carotene, contributing antioxidant activity and vitamin A precursor content.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Health Benefits

## Immune Support

Very high vitamin C content supports normal immune system function.

## Iron Absorption

Vitamin C enhances the absorption of non-haem iron from plant foods when eaten together, useful for vegetarians and vegans managing iron intake.

## Eye Health

Carotenoid content, particularly in red and yellow varieties, contributes to normal vision and eye health.

# Allergen Information

Bell peppers are not one of the fourteen major EU/UK allergens, though they belong to the nightshade family and mild oral allergy syndrome can occur in individuals with certain pollen sensitivities.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Contraindications

**Avoid if:**

-   Confirmed bell pepper allergy (uncommon)

**Use caution if:**

-   Nightshade sensitivity is suspected; while robust clinical evidence for widespread nightshade intolerance is limited, individuals who notice symptoms can discuss an elimination approach with a healthcare provider or dietitian

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Practical Guidance

## Buying

Choose:

-   Firm peppers with smooth, unwrinkled skin
-   Red or yellow varieties for the highest vitamin C and carotenoid content, if colour flexibility allows

## Storage

Store in the refrigerator; use within about a week for best quality.

## Preparation

Suitable raw in salads and with dips, or cooked in stir-fries, roasted, or stuffed; some vitamin C is lost with prolonged cooking, so lighter cooking or raw preparation preserves more.

# Common Myth

**Myth:** All bell pepper colours are nutritionally identical, just different varieties.

**Fact:** Green, yellow, orange and red bell peppers are typically the same fruit at different stages of ripeness (green being least ripe), and vitamin C and carotenoid content generally increase as the pepper ripens and reddens.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Key Takeaway

Bell peppers, especially red varieties, are an exceptionally rich vegetable source of vitamin C and carotenoids, supporting immune function and enhancing iron absorption from plant-based meals.
