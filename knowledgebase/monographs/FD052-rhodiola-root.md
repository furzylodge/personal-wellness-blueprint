# FD052 - Rhodiola Root

**Knowledge Base ID:** FD052\
**Scientific Name:** *Rhodiola rosea*\
**Common Names:** Rhodiola, Golden Root, Arctic Root\
**Food Group:** Herbs & Spices (FG006)\
**Evidence Level:** Moderate\
**Status:** Approved

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Overview

Rhodiola rosea is a root traditionally used in Scandinavian and Russian folk medicine, and is one of the more clinically studied botanicals classified as an adaptogen. Research has focused particularly on fatigue and mental performance under stress, with a moderate but genuine body of human trial evidence compared with many other adaptogens.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Why It Matters

Rhodiola stands out among adaptogenic botanicals for having a comparatively better-developed human clinical evidence base:

-   Multiple randomised controlled trials have examined Rhodiola extract for fatigue and stress-related symptoms
-   Contains rosavins and salidroside, compounds unique to Rhodiola studied for their proposed stress-modulating activity
-   Generally well-tolerated in studied dosing ranges, though evidence quality and standardisation across trials varies
-   Typically used as a standardised extract rather than eaten as a whole food

# Active Compounds

## Salidroside and Rosavins

Compounds relatively unique to Rhodiola rosea, studied for proposed effects on stress-hormone regulation and cellular energy metabolism; several human trials using standardised extracts have reported improvements in fatigue symptoms and subjective stress measures, though trial sizes are generally modest and standardisation between products varies.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Health Benefits

## Fatigue and Stress Support

Rhodiola has one of the more substantial human clinical evidence bases among adaptogens for reducing symptoms of fatigue and improving subjective measures of stress and mental performance under demanding conditions, though effect sizes are generally modest and further high-quality research would strengthen confidence in these findings.

## Mental Performance Under Stress

Some trials suggest improvements in cognitive performance measures during periods of fatigue or stress, an area of ongoing research interest.

# Allergen Information

Rhodiola is not one of the fourteen major EU/UK allergens; allergy is rare but not well characterised in the literature.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Contraindications

**Avoid if:**

-   Pregnant or breastfeeding, as safety data in these populations is insufficient
-   Bipolar disorder, without medical supervision, as some case reports suggest a theoretical risk of triggering manic episodes given Rhodiola's stimulating properties

**Use caution if:**

-   Taking antidepressant or other mood-affecting medication, given a theoretical potential for interaction; discuss with a healthcare provider or pharmacist first
-   Sensitive to stimulating effects, as some users report initial restlessness or sleep disturbance, particularly if taken later in the day

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Practical Guidance

## Buying

Choose:

-   Standardised extract products (commonly standardised to rosavin and salidroside content) from reputable suppliers, as unstandardised products vary widely in potency

## Storage

Store extracts/capsules in a cool, dry place per packaging guidance.

## Preparation

Typically taken as a standardised capsule or tincture extract rather than prepared as a food; not commonly used in cooking.

# Common Myth

**Myth:** Rhodiola works the same way and as reliably as caffeine for energy.

**Fact:** Rhodiola's studied effects relate more to reducing fatigue and stress symptoms over sustained use rather than providing an immediate stimulant effect like caffeine, and its evidence base, while comparatively good among adaptogens, is still smaller and more variable than that for well-established dietary compounds.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Key Takeaway

Rhodiola rosea is one of the better-researched adaptogenic botanicals, with a genuine, if modest, body of human clinical evidence for fatigue and stress-related symptom support, alongside real considerations around medication interactions and use in certain mental health conditions.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Evidence Summary

**Overall Evidence Rating:** Moderate

Supported by:

-   Multiple randomised controlled trials on Rhodiola rosea extract and fatigue/stress outcomes
-   Systematic reviews of adaptogen clinical research
-   Traditional use documentation (Scandinavian and Russian folk medicine)

Evidence caveat: trial sizes are generally modest and extract standardisation varies between studies; this monograph should be read as reflecting a moderately, rather than firmly, established evidence base.
