# FD081 - Barley

**Knowledge Base ID:** FD081\
**Scientific Name:** *Hordeum vulgare*\
**Common Names:** Barley, Pearl Barley, Pot Barley\
**Food Group:** Wholegrains (FG001)\
**Evidence Level:** High\
**Status:** Approved

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Overview

Barley is a wholegrain cereal notable, like oats, for its beta-glucan soluble fibre content. It has an established evidence base and an EFSA-authorised health claim relating to cholesterol reduction.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Why It Matters

Regular barley consumption is associated with:

-   Meaningful beta-glucan soluble fibre intake, supporting cholesterol management
-   Improved satiety and slower digestion of carbohydrate
-   Contribution to overall fibre and manganese intake

Note that pearl barley (the most commonly available form) has had some of its bran layer removed, giving it somewhat less fibre than hulled (pot) barley, though it retains meaningful beta-glucan content.

# Active Compounds

## Beta-glucan

A soluble fibre shown in multiple studies to help lower LDL cholesterol when consumed regularly as part of the diet; the European Food Safety Authority has authorised a health claim for barley beta-glucan and cholesterol reduction.

## Manganese and Selenium

Barley is a good source of both, supporting antioxidant enzyme function and metabolic processes.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Health Benefits

## Cardiovascular Health

Beta-glucan content supports healthy LDL cholesterol levels as part of a balanced diet, with an EFSA-authorised health claim to this effect.

## Digestive Health

Fibre content supports bowel regularity and feeds beneficial gut bacteria.

## Blood Sugar Management

Beta-glucan slows carbohydrate digestion, contributing to a more gradual rise in blood glucose after meals.

# Allergen Information

Barley contains gluten, one of the fourteen major EU/UK allergens requiring mandatory labelling.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Contraindications

**Avoid if:**

-   Coeliac disease or non-coeliac gluten sensitivity
-   Confirmed barley/gluten allergy

**Use caution if:**

-   Introducing high-fibre foods to individuals with sensitive digestion

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Practical Guidance

## Buying

Choose:

-   Hulled or pot barley for the most complete wholegrain, retaining more bran and fibre than pearl barley
-   Pearl barley as a quicker-cooking, still fibre-containing alternative

## Storage

Store in an airtight container in a cool, dry place.

## Preparation

Suitable in soups, stews, salads, and as a rice substitute; typically requires longer cooking than rice, particularly hulled barley.

# Common Myth

**Myth:** All barley products are equally wholegrain.

**Fact:** Pearl barley has had some of its bran layer polished away and so retains less fibre than hulled (pot) barley, though it is still a useful fibre source compared with refined grains.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Key Takeaway

Barley is a wholegrain cereal with a well-established, EFSA-recognised role in cholesterol management through its beta-glucan content, though it is not suitable for gluten-free diets.
