# FD065 - Sesame Seeds

**Knowledge Base ID:** FD065\
**Scientific Name:** *Sesamum indicum*\
**Common Names:** Sesame Seeds\
**Food Group:** Nuts & Seeds (FG005)\
**Evidence Level:** High\
**Status:** Approved

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Overview

Sesame seeds are a small oilseed valued for their calcium content, healthy fat profile, and unique lignan compounds (sesamin and sesamolin). They are also one of the fourteen major EU/UK allergens requiring mandatory labelling.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Why It Matters

Sesame seeds offer a distinctive nutrient profile among seeds:

-   One of the richest plant sources of calcium, relevant for those limiting dairy
-   Good source of healthy unsaturated fats, copper and manganese
-   Contains sesamin and sesamolin, lignans studied for antioxidant and cholesterol-related effects
-   A recognised major allergen requiring careful attention for those with sesame allergy

# Active Compounds

## Sesamin and Sesamolin

Lignans relatively unique to sesame, studied for antioxidant properties and a modest cholesterol-lowering effect in some clinical research, though effect sizes are generally small.

## Calcium

Sesame seeds (particularly unhulled) are a notably rich plant source of calcium, useful for those with limited dairy intake, though the calcium is bound with oxalates that somewhat reduce its absorption compared with dairy calcium.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Health Benefits

## Bone Health Support

Calcium and manganese content contribute to bone health, particularly valuable within plant-based diets.

## Cardiovascular Health

Sesame's unsaturated fat and lignan content have been associated in some clinical research with modest favourable effects on blood lipid profiles.

# Allergen Information

Sesame is one of the fourteen major EU/UK allergens requiring mandatory labelling, and sesame allergy can be severe; cross-contamination in bakery and manufactured products is a recognised concern.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Contraindications

**Avoid if:**

-   Confirmed or suspected sesame allergy

**Use caution if:**

-   Relying on sesame calcium as a primary calcium source without dietary variety, given its somewhat lower bioavailability compared with dairy calcium due to oxalate content

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Practical Guidance

## Buying

Choose:

-   Unhulled sesame seeds for a higher calcium and fibre content than hulled varieties
-   Products clearly labelled if sesame-free environments are required for allergy reasons

## Storage

Store in an airtight container in a cool, dark place, or refrigerate/freeze for extended freshness, as sesame's oil content can turn rancid over time.

## Preparation

Suitable sprinkled on salads and baked goods, ground into tahini, or used in dressings, hummus, and Middle Eastern and Asian cuisines.

# Common Myth

**Myth:** Sesame allergy is rare and not a serious concern.

**Fact:** Sesame allergy has become increasingly recognised as a significant food allergy, serious enough that it was added to the list of fourteen major allergens requiring mandatory labelling in the UK and EU, and can cause severe reactions in sensitive individuals.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Key Takeaway

Sesame seeds are a valuable plant-based source of calcium and unique lignan antioxidants, but must be treated with the same allergen seriousness as other major allergens like peanuts and tree nuts.
