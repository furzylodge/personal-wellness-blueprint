# FD066 - Sesame Oil

**Knowledge Base ID:** FD066\
**Scientific Name:** *Sesamum indicum* (oil)\
**Common Names:** Sesame Oil\
**Food Group:** Oils & Fats (FG012)\
**Evidence Level:** Moderate\
**Status:** Approved

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Overview

Sesame oil is extracted from sesame seeds and comes in two culinarily distinct forms: a light, refined version with a mild flavour suited to general cooking, and a toasted (dark) version with an intense nutty flavour used primarily as a flavouring rather than a cooking oil.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Why It Matters

Sesame oil retains some, though not all, of the nutritional character of whole sesame seeds:

-   Predominantly unsaturated fat, with a mix of monounsaturated and polyunsaturated fatty acids
-   Retains sesamin and sesamol, antioxidant lignan-related compounds, particularly in less refined versions
-   Toasted sesame oil is used in small quantities as a flavouring, so its overall nutrient contribution to the diet is modest
-   Refined sesame oil has a relatively high smoke point suitable for general cooking

# Active Compounds

## Sesamol and Sesamin

Antioxidant compounds carried over from the sesame seed during oil extraction, present in varying amounts depending on refining and toasting; toasted sesame oil generally retains more of these compounds and its distinctive flavour than refined sesame oil.

## Unsaturated Fatty Acids

Sesame oil is predominantly composed of monounsaturated and polyunsaturated fats, associated with more favourable cardiovascular effects than saturated fat sources when used in place of them.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Health Benefits

## Cardiovascular Health

As with other predominantly unsaturated oils, using sesame oil in place of saturated fat sources is associated with more favourable blood lipid profiles.

## Antioxidant Contribution

Sesamol and related compounds contribute modest antioxidant activity, particularly in less refined or toasted forms.

# Allergen Information

Sesame oil is derived from sesame, one of the fourteen major EU/UK allergens requiring mandatory labelling; even refined sesame oil can retain allergenic protein and should be avoided by those with sesame allergy unless specifically confirmed safe by an allergy specialist.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Contraindications

**Avoid if:**

-   Confirmed or suspected sesame allergy

**Use caution if:**

-   Using toasted sesame oil for high-heat cooking, as its lower smoke point and delicate flavour compounds make it better suited as a finishing oil rather than a primary cooking fat

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Practical Guidance

## Buying

Choose:

-   Refined, light sesame oil for general cooking at higher heat
-   Toasted (dark) sesame oil for flavouring dressings, marinades, and finished dishes

## Storage

Store in a cool, dark place; refrigeration can extend shelf life, particularly for toasted sesame oil.

## Preparation

Suitable for stir-frying (refined) or as a finishing flavour drizzle (toasted) in Asian-influenced cooking, dressings, and marinades.

# Common Myth

**Myth:** All sesame oil is the same and can be used interchangeably.

**Fact:** Refined (light) and toasted (dark) sesame oil differ substantially in flavour intensity, smoke point, and typical culinary use; toasted sesame oil is generally used sparingly as a flavouring rather than a general cooking oil.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Key Takeaway

Sesame oil provides a predominantly unsaturated fat profile with some antioxidant lignan content, and works best in the kitchen when the refined and toasted varieties are used for their distinct culinary purposes.
