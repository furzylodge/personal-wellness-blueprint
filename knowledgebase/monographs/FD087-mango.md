# FD087 - Mango

**Knowledge Base ID:** FD087\
**Scientific Name:** *Mangifera indica*\
**Common Names:** Mango\
**Food Group:** Fruit (FG003)\
**Evidence Level:** High\
**Status:** Approved

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Overview

Mango is a tropical fruit notable for its high vitamin C and vitamin A (as beta-carotene) content, along with a range of polyphenols. It is naturally sweet with a relatively high sugar content compared with many other fruits.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Why It Matters

Mango offers a particularly strong micronutrient profile among fruits:

-   Excellent source of vitamin C, supporting immune function and collagen synthesis
-   Rich in beta-carotene, a precursor to vitamin A, supporting eye and immune health
-   Contains polyphenols including mangiferin, an antioxidant compound of research interest
-   Good source of fibre and folate

# Active Compounds

## Beta-carotene

A carotenoid pigment responsible for mango's orange colour and a precursor to vitamin A in the body, supporting eye health, immune function and skin integrity.

## Vitamin C

Mango is a rich source, supporting immune function, iron absorption and collagen formation.

## Mangiferin

A polyphenol relatively unique to mango, studied in preclinical research for antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties; human clinical evidence at typical dietary intakes remains limited.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Health Benefits

## Immune Support

High vitamin C and beta-carotene content support normal immune system function.

## Eye Health

Vitamin A (from beta-carotene conversion) plays an established role in maintaining normal vision.

## Digestive Health

Fibre content supports bowel regularity, and mango has been studied for a mild prebiotic effect on gut bacteria.

# Allergen Information

Mango is not one of the fourteen major EU/UK allergens, though it belongs to the same plant family as cashews and pistachios (Anacardiaceae) and its skin contains urushiol-related compounds that can cause contact dermatitis in sensitive individuals, particularly those with a latex or poison ivy sensitivity.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Contraindications

**Avoid if:**

-   Confirmed mango allergy or known sensitivity to mango skin contact

**Use caution if:**

-   Managing blood sugar closely, as mango has a moderately high natural sugar content compared with many other fruits; portion awareness is sensible

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Practical Guidance

## Buying

Choose:

-   Mangoes that yield slightly to gentle pressure when ripe; colour varies by variety and is not always a reliable ripeness indicator

## Storage

Ripen at room temperature, then refrigerate ripe mango and use within a few days; can also be frozen once cut.

## Preparation

Suitable eaten fresh, sliced into salads, blended into smoothies, or used in salsas and chutneys; wash and avoid contact with skin sap when preparing if sensitive.

# Common Myth

**Myth:** Mango is too sugary to be part of a healthy diet.

**Fact:** While mango has a higher natural sugar content than some other fruits, it delivers this alongside substantial fibre, vitamin C and vitamin A, and fits well within a balanced diet in normal portions.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Key Takeaway

Mango is a nutrient-dense tropical fruit offering an excellent combination of vitamin C and vitamin A, making it a valuable contributor to immune and eye health as part of a varied diet.
