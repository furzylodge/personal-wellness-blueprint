# FD067 - Soybeans

**Knowledge Base ID:** FD067\
**Scientific Name:** *Glycine max*\
**Common Names:** Soybeans, Soya Beans, Edamame (immature form)\
**Food Group:** Beans & Legumes (FG004)\
**Evidence Level:** High\
**Status:** Approved

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Overview

Soybeans are a legume notable for being one of the few plant foods providing a complete protein, and are the source ingredient for tofu, tempeh, soy milk and other soy-based foods. They naturally contain isoflavones, plant compounds studied extensively for their potential health effects.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Why It Matters

Soybeans have an unusually strong and well-studied nutritional profile among legumes:

-   One of the few plant foods providing a complete protein, containing all essential amino acids
-   Good source of fibre, folate, and manganese
-   Contain isoflavones (genistein, daidzein), studied for roles in cardiovascular health and menopausal symptom management
-   Basis for numerous minimally and more processed soy foods with varying nutritional profiles

# Active Compounds

## Complete Protein

Soybeans contain all nine essential amino acids in useful proportions, unusual among legumes and valuable within vegetarian and vegan diets seeking complete plant protein sources.

## Isoflavones (Genistein, Daidzein)

Plant compounds structurally similar to oestrogen, extensively studied for potential roles in cardiovascular health and menopausal symptom management; overall research, including large reviews, has generally found soy isoflavones safe at typical dietary intakes, with modest and mixed evidence of specific clinical benefit.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Health Benefits

## Plant-Based Protein Support

Soybeans' complete protein profile makes them a particularly valuable protein source within vegetarian and vegan dietary patterns.

## Cardiovascular Health

Soy protein has been associated in some research with modest improvements in LDL cholesterol when substituted for animal protein, supporting an EFSA-recognised health claim for soy protein and cholesterol.

## Digestive Health

Fibre content supports bowel regularity and feeds beneficial gut bacteria.

# Allergen Information

Soy (soybeans) is one of the fourteen major EU/UK allergens requiring mandatory labelling.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Contraindications

**Avoid if:**

-   Confirmed soy allergy

**Use caution if:**

-   A history of hormone-sensitive conditions raises questions about isoflavone intake; individual clinical advice is recommended in this case rather than a blanket restriction, as current evidence does not support a general contraindication for typical dietary intake
-   Taking thyroid medication, as soy has been noted to potentially affect thyroid hormone absorption if consumed at the same time; separating intake by a few hours is generally advised

# Practical Guidance

## Buying

Choose:

-   Dried soybeans for cooking from scratch, or tinned/cooked soybeans for convenience
-   Edamame (immature soybeans) as a snack or side dish option

## Storage

Store dried soybeans in an airtight container in a cool, dry place; cooked soybeans should be refrigerated and used within a few days.

## Preparation

Requires thorough cooking (dried soybeans need soaking and prolonged boiling) before eating; also the base ingredient for tofu, tempeh, soy milk, and miso.

# Common Myth

**Myth:** Soy isoflavones act as a strong hormone disruptor at typical dietary intakes.

**Fact:** Current evidence from human studies at typical dietary consumption levels does not support meaningful hormonal disruption; isoflavones have much weaker oestrogenic activity than the body's own oestrogen, and major health bodies consider moderate soy food consumption safe for the general population.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Key Takeaway

Soybeans are one of the most nutritionally complete legumes available, providing a rare plant-based complete protein along with isoflavones that have a well-studied, generally reassuring safety profile at typical dietary intakes.
