# FD083 - Peanuts

**Knowledge Base ID:** FD083\
**Scientific Name:** *Arachis hypogaea*\
**Common Names:** Peanuts, Groundnuts\
**Food Group:** Nuts & Seeds (FG005)\
**Evidence Level:** High\
**Status:** Approved

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Overview

Peanuts are technically a legume (a seed that grows underground in a pod) but are nutritionally and culinarily grouped with nuts due to their similar fat, protein and micronutrient profile. They are an affordable, energy-dense source of unsaturated fat, plant protein and several key micronutrients.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Why It Matters

Peanuts are one of the most extensively studied nuts (or nut-like legumes) in cardiovascular nutrition research:

-   Predominantly unsaturated fat, associated in large cohort studies with reduced cardiovascular risk when replacing saturated fat
-   Good source of plant protein and fibre
-   Rich in niacin, vitamin E and biotin
-   Contains resveratrol, though at lower concentrations than in some other sources

# Active Compounds

## Unsaturated Fatty Acids

Peanuts are predominantly composed of monounsaturated and polyunsaturated fats, associated with favourable effects on blood lipid profiles when used to replace saturated fat sources.

## Resveratrol

A polyphenol found in small amounts in peanuts (and better known from red grapes/wine), studied for antioxidant properties, though dietary peanut intake provides much lower amounts than research doses.

## Niacin and Vitamin E

Peanuts are a particularly good source of both, supporting energy metabolism and antioxidant defence respectively.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Health Benefits

## Cardiovascular Health

Multiple large cohort studies (including Harvard-affiliated research) associate regular nut consumption, including peanuts, with lower risk of cardiovascular disease, attributed to their unsaturated fat and nutrient profile.

## Weight Management

Despite being energy-dense, peanuts are associated in observational studies with satiety and, when eaten in moderation as part of a balanced diet, are not consistently linked with weight gain.

## Blood Sugar Management

Peanuts have a low glycaemic index and their fat, protein and fibre content contribute to a moderated glycaemic response when eaten alongside carbohydrate.

# Allergen Information

Peanuts are one of the fourteen major EU/UK allergens requiring mandatory labelling, and peanut allergy is among the most common and potentially severe food allergies. Cross-contamination is a significant concern in manufactured foods.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Contraindications

**Avoid if:**

-   Confirmed or suspected peanut allergy

**Use caution if:**

-   Introducing to infants; current UK guidance (NHS/BDA) supports early, age-appropriate introduction of peanut in infants without existing allergy to help reduce allergy risk, but this should follow official guidance, particularly where eczema or existing egg allergy is present, and be discussed with a health visitor or GP

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Practical Guidance

## Buying

Choose:

-   Unsalted, dry-roasted varieties to limit added salt
-   Products clearly labelled if peanut-free environments are required for allergy reasons

## Storage

Store in an airtight container in a cool, dry place; refrigerate or freeze for longer shelf life, as peanuts can turn rancid.

## Preparation

Suitable eaten whole as a snack, in peanut butter, satay sauces, and as a garnish in various cuisines.

# Common Myth

**Myth:** Peanuts are true nuts.

**Fact:** Peanuts are legumes that grow underground in pods, botanically distinct from tree nuts like almonds or walnuts, though they share a broadly similar nutritional profile and are grouped with nuts for dietary purposes.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Key Takeaway

Peanuts are an affordable, nutrient-dense legume with a strong evidence base linking regular, moderate consumption to cardiovascular benefit, though they are a leading cause of serious food allergy.
