# FD080 - Quinoa

**Knowledge Base ID:** FD080\
**Scientific Name:** *Chenopodium quinoa*\
**Common Names:** Quinoa\
**Food Group:** Wholegrains (FG001)\
**Evidence Level:** High\
**Status:** Approved

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Overview

Quinoa is a seed typically prepared and consumed like a wholegrain. It is notable among plant foods for being a complete protein, containing all nine essential amino acids, and is naturally gluten-free.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Why It Matters

Quinoa's nutritional profile makes it a particularly valuable wholegrain-style food, especially within plant-based diets:

-   One of the few plant foods providing a complete protein
-   Naturally gluten-free
-   Good source of magnesium, iron and manganese
-   Higher fibre content than many refined grains

# Active Compounds

## Complete Protein

Quinoa contains all nine essential amino acids in useful proportions, unusual among plant foods and particularly valuable for plant-based diets seeking complete protein sources.

## Saponins

Naturally occurring compounds in quinoa's outer coating with a bitter taste; largely removed during commercial processing and by rinsing before cooking.

## Iron and Manganese

Quinoa is a good source of both, though as with other plant sources, the non-haem iron is less bioavailable than iron from animal sources; pairing with vitamin C-rich foods can improve absorption.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Health Benefits

## Plant-Based Protein Support

Its complete amino acid profile makes quinoa a valuable protein source within vegetarian and vegan dietary patterns.

## Digestive Health

Higher fibre content than refined grains supports bowel regularity and satiety.

## Blood Sugar Management

Quinoa's fibre and protein content contribute to a comparatively moderate glycaemic response relative to refined grains.

# Allergen Information

Quinoa is naturally gluten-free and is not one of the fourteen major EU/UK allergens, though allergy, while rare, can occur.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Contraindications

**Avoid if:**

-   Confirmed quinoa allergy

**Use caution if:**

-   Not rinsed before cooking, as residual saponins can impart a bitter taste and may cause mild digestive discomfort in sensitive individuals

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Practical Guidance

## Buying

Choose:

-   Pre-rinsed or "ready to cook" varieties, or rinse thoroughly before cooking
-   White, red or black quinoa, which differ slightly in texture but are nutritionally similar

## Storage

Store uncooked quinoa in an airtight container in a cool, dry place; refrigerate cooked quinoa and use within a few days.

## Preparation

Rinse thoroughly before cooking to remove saponins. Suitable as a rice substitute, in salads, bowls, and as a side dish.

# Common Myth

**Myth:** Quinoa is a grain.

**Fact:** Quinoa is technically a seed from a plant related to spinach and beetroot, but it is prepared and consumed like a wholegrain, so is conventionally grouped with wholegrains in dietary guidance.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Key Takeaway

Quinoa is a nutrient-dense, naturally gluten-free seed valued for being one of the few complete plant proteins, making it particularly useful within vegetarian and vegan diets.
