# FD085 - Bananas

**Knowledge Base ID:** FD085\
**Scientific Name:** *Musa acuminata*\
**Common Names:** Bananas\
**Food Group:** Fruit (FG003)\
**Evidence Level:** High\
**Status:** Approved

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Overview

Bananas are one of the most widely consumed fruits globally, notable for their potassium content, vitamin B6, and a carbohydrate profile that shifts from resistant starch to natural sugars as the fruit ripens.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Why It Matters

Bananas offer a distinctive nutrient profile among fruits:

-   One of the richest common fruit sources of potassium, relevant to blood pressure regulation
-   Good source of vitamin B6
-   Unripe/green bananas contain resistant starch, which behaves like fibre in the gut
-   Convenient, pre-packaged, and widely accessible energy source

# Active Compounds

## Potassium

Bananas are a well-known dietary source of potassium, an electrolyte with an established role in blood pressure regulation, particularly as part of a diet with reduced sodium intake.

## Resistant Starch

Present in higher amounts in unripe (green) bananas, resistant starch passes through the small intestine undigested and is fermented in the colon, behaving similarly to fibre and supporting gut bacteria; this content decreases as the banana ripens and starches convert to sugars.

## Vitamin B6

Involved in amino acid metabolism and neurotransmitter synthesis; bananas are a useful dietary source.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Health Benefits

## Blood Pressure Support

Regular potassium intake, including from bananas, is associated with healthy blood pressure levels, particularly when replacing lower-potassium, higher-sodium food choices.

## Digestive Health

Fibre and, in less ripe bananas, resistant starch support digestive health and beneficial gut bacteria.

## Exercise Recovery

Bananas' combination of easily digestible carbohydrate and potassium has made them a popular, evidence-supported choice for pre- or post-exercise fuelling.

# Allergen Information

Bananas are not one of the fourteen major EU/UK allergens, though a latex-fruit cross-reactivity syndrome exists in some individuals with latex allergy.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Contraindications

**Avoid if:**

-   Confirmed banana allergy or latex-fruit syndrome affecting banana

**Use caution if:**

-   Following a potassium-restricted diet (e.g. some chronic kidney disease management plans), where portion size and frequency should be discussed with a healthcare provider or dietitian

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Practical Guidance

## Buying

Choose:

-   Riper bananas (more yellow, with spots) for a sweeter taste and more readily available sugars
-   Greener bananas for higher resistant starch content and a firmer texture

## Storage

Store at room temperature; ripening can be slowed by refrigeration (skin may darken but flesh stays fine) or accelerated by storing near other ripening fruit.

## Preparation

Suitable eaten whole, sliced into cereal, blended into smoothies, or baked into breads and cakes.

# Common Myth

**Myth:** Bananas are too high in sugar to be a healthy choice.

**Fact:** Bananas contain natural sugars alongside fibre, potassium and micronutrients, and their overall glycaemic impact and health associations compare favourably with many other carbohydrate-containing snack foods; ripeness affects sugar content and glycaemic response.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Key Takeaway

Bananas are a convenient, potassium-rich fruit with well-established relevance to blood pressure support and a versatile role in digestive and exercise-related nutrition.
