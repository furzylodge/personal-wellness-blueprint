# FD072 - Lean Beef

**Knowledge Base ID:** FD072\
**Scientific Name:** *Bos taurus*\
**Common Names:** Lean Beef, Lean Red Meat\
**Food Group:** Meat & Poultry (FG008)\
**Evidence Level:** High\
**Status:** Approved

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Overview

Lean beef refers to cuts of beef with the visible fat trimmed away, providing a rich source of highly bioavailable iron, zinc and vitamin B12, alongside complete protein. It is one of the most nutrient-dense sources of these particular micronutrients in the diet.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Why It Matters

Moderate consumption of lean beef contributes to:

-   High-quality complete protein intake
-   Highly bioavailable (haem) iron intake, relevant to preventing iron-deficiency anaemia
-   Zinc intake, supporting immune function and wound healing
-   Vitamin B12 intake, essential for nerve function and red blood cell formation

Public health guidance in the UK and elsewhere generally recommends moderating intake of red and processed meat rather than eliminating lean red meat, given its contribution of iron, zinc and B12 which can be harder to obtain in equivalent bioavailable forms from plant sources alone.

# Active Compounds

## Haem Iron

The form of iron found in red meat, absorbed considerably more efficiently by the body than the non-haem iron found in plant foods.

## Zinc

Supports immune function, wound healing and protein synthesis.

## Vitamin B12

Essential for red blood cell formation, neurological function and DNA synthesis; naturally found almost exclusively in animal foods.

## Creatine

A naturally occurring compound in muscle tissue that contributes to short-term energy supply in muscle cells.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Health Benefits

## Prevention of Iron-Deficiency Anaemia

The highly bioavailable haem iron in lean beef supports healthy iron status, particularly relevant for population groups at higher risk of deficiency (e.g. menstruating women).

## Muscle Maintenance

Complete protein content supports muscle protein synthesis and maintenance.

## Neurological Health

Vitamin B12 content supports normal nervous system function.

# Allergen Information

Beef is not one of the recognised major food allergens, though allergy, while rare, can occur.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Contraindications

**Avoid if:**

-   Confirmed beef/red meat allergy

**Use caution if:**

-   Following dietary guidance to limit red meat intake for cardiovascular or colorectal health reasons; current public health guidance (e.g. UK SACN) generally recommends limiting red and processed meat intake to no more than 70g/day on average
-   Processed beef products (e.g. cured, smoked) are consumed frequently, due to their association with higher colorectal cancer risk compared with fresh unprocessed meat

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Practical Guidance

## Buying

Choose:

-   Lean cuts (e.g. sirloin, round, lean mince) with visible fat trimmed
-   Fresh, unprocessed beef over cured or processed products

## Storage

Refrigerate raw beef and use within the use-by date, or freeze for longer storage.

## Preparation

Cook thoroughly to a safe internal temperature. Suitable for grilling, roasting, stir-frying and stewing.

# Common Myth

**Myth:** All red meat carries the same health risks.

**Fact:** Most of the evidence linking red meat consumption to adverse health outcomes relates most strongly to processed meat and very high intakes; moderate consumption of lean, unprocessed beef within current public health guidance is treated differently from processed meat in most dietary recommendations.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Key Takeaway

Lean beef is a concentrated source of highly bioavailable iron, zinc and vitamin B12, useful within a balanced diet in moderation and in line with current public health guidance on red meat intake.
