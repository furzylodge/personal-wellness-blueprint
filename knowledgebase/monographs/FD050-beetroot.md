# FD050 - Beetroot

**Knowledge Base ID:** FD050\
**Scientific Name:** *Beta vulgaris*\
**Common Names:** Beetroot, Beets\
**Food Group:** Vegetables (FG002)\
**Evidence Level:** High\
**Status:** Approved

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Overview

Beetroot is a root vegetable notable for its high dietary nitrate content, which the body converts into nitric oxide, a molecule involved in blood vessel dilation. This has made beetroot one of the most researched vegetables in sports nutrition and cardiovascular research.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Why It Matters

Beetroot's nitrate content underpins a substantial and still-growing body of research:

-   Dietary nitrate is converted to nitric oxide, supporting blood vessel dilation and blood flow
-   Studied extensively in relation to blood pressure and exercise performance
-   Source of folate, manganese, and potassium
-   Contains betalains, distinctive antioxidant pigments responsible for its deep red colour

# Active Compounds

## Dietary Nitrate

Converted in the body to nitric oxide via the nitrate-nitrite-nitric oxide pathway, supporting vasodilation; beetroot juice supplementation has shown effects on blood pressure and exercise performance in a substantial body of clinical research.

## Betalains

Pigments responsible for beetroot's characteristic colour, with antioxidant properties studied in preclinical and some human research.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Health Benefits

## Blood Pressure Support

Multiple clinical trials have found that beetroot juice or dietary nitrate intake can produce modest, short-term reductions in blood pressure in some individuals.

## Exercise Performance

Beetroot's nitrate content has been studied extensively in athletic populations for potential improvements in oxygen efficiency and exercise tolerance, particularly in endurance activities.

## Antioxidant Support

Betalain pigments contribute to overall dietary antioxidant intake.

# Allergen Information

Beetroot is not one of the fourteen major EU/UK allergens; allergy is rare.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Contraindications

**Avoid if:**

-   Confirmed beetroot allergy (rare)
-   A history of certain types of kidney stones (calcium oxalate), where a low-oxalate diet has been advised, as beetroot is relatively high in oxalates

**Use caution if:**

-   Taking blood pressure-lowering medication, as nitrate-rich beetroot may have an additive effect; discuss with a healthcare provider if relevant
-   Experiencing beeturia (pink/red urine or stool after eating beetroot); this is a harmless, genetically determined response in some individuals and not a sign of a health problem

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Practical Guidance

## Buying

Choose:

-   Fresh beetroot with firm skin, or ready-cooked (vacuum-packed) beetroot without added vinegar if a milder flavour or nitrate benefit is the goal
-   Beetroot juice specifically marketed for nitrate content if using for exercise performance purposes

## Storage

Store fresh, unwashed beetroot in the refrigerator; cooked or vacuum-packed beetroot per packaging guidance.

## Preparation

Suitable roasted, boiled, grated raw into salads, or juiced; cooking method has some effect on nitrate retention, with boiling causing more nitrate loss into cooking water than roasting or steaming.

# Common Myth

**Myth:** Pink or red urine after eating beetroot is a sign of a health problem.

**Fact:** This harmless phenomenon, known as beeturia, occurs in a genetically determined subset of the population who don't fully break down beetroot's red betalain pigments; it is not a sign of illness, though new unexplained changes in urine colour without a known dietary cause should always be checked with a doctor.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Key Takeaway

Beetroot's dietary nitrate content is one of the most well-researched vegetable compounds in cardiovascular and sports nutrition science, with a genuine, well-evidenced (if often modest) effect on blood pressure and exercise performance.
